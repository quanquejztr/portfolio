## 1. The idea
One dark field from top to bottom, and in it, lit things. Photographs and objects appear like exhibits in a dim gallery: each with space around it, each the brightest thing on its screen. Type is light, small and sparse. The darkness is not a theme switch; it is a room, and the page is a slow walk through it. It suits subjects sold on atmosphere and material: an evening restaurant, a metal card, a record, a house at dusk.

Before anything else, write one sentence in your notes: "On this page, <the subject's own material> becomes <the device>."
Fill the first blank from your inventory of what only this client has (its name and what it means, its street, its numbers, its
constraint), then put the nearest competitor in the sentence: if it still works, the idea is the category's, not the client's.
Then name the one live thing the visitor can do on the page (SKILL.md step 4).

## 2. Composition
A single near-black or deep-toned ground runs the full page with no section bands. First screen: either the name at full width in a light weight along the bottom edge with one dim photograph above it, or one object or photograph centred at 40–50% of the viewport with a line of text beneath at 16–18px. Each later screen holds one or two lit items at a generous size (the main one 40–60% of the screen width) with its text set beside it at full reading size; the darkness frames the items, it does not replace content. Images vary in size but all share the same darkness at their edges so they sit in the ground without frames. Practical details come last in a simple two-column text block. The only solid light element on the page is the single primary action.

## 3. Type
Kept from the brand. Light or regular weights; they glow on dark. Avoid heavy bold, which looks cheap on black. Headings 4–7vw at most; text 16–18px with leading 1.6 and a slightly lifted ink (not pure white).

## 4. Colour
Kept from the brand. A dark ground with a tint (blue-black, green-black, brown-black), a soft light ink, a dimmer ink for secondary text, and one warm or bright accent used once per screen at most.

Low-key photographs: dark backgrounds, one light source, subjects emerging from shadow. Their dark edges should blend into the page ground.

## 6. Hero — Title from the top edge to the bottom edge
One full-bleed photograph or film at 100vw by 100svh, with a horizon near half height and empty sky or darkness above. The headline is two lines in a heavy face at clamp(140px, 13vw, 200px), leading 0.85, pulled as far apart as the screen allows: line one flush to the 32px left margin with its cap line 72px from the top; line two indented 28vw (403px) and anchored to the bottom, its baseline 24px above the lower edge, running towards the right margin. Lay the hero out as a grid of three rows (`grid-template-rows: auto 1fr auto`): line one in the first row, line two in the third, both spans of one `h1` with `display: contents`. The middle row leaves the picture clear apart from one supporting sentence at 18px, max 44ch, crossing the horizon at 48% of the height from 30vw, and the single action directly beneath it as a solid rectangular button with its label at 18px. The wordmark and two text links sit top-right on the 32px margin at 15px; the top-left belongs to line one. Choose a picture whose upper-left and lower-right are calm enough for the type, or lay a neutral scrim under those two corners only.

On a phone: Keep the two lines at 19vw: line one flush left on a 16px margin 88px down, line two right-aligned to the 16px margin with its baseline 20px above the bottom. The sentence at 17px and the action button sit between them on the left margin, at 46% of the height. The picture is re-cropped to portrait around the horizon.
Headline: Two words or short phrases, one per line, at most about 8 characters each.

## 7. Sections, in order
Each is a composition to rebuild with this project's content. Keep the geometry; change the subject. Re-order or drop a
section when the argument of the page needs it (see references/copy.md), never to fall back to a card grid.

### 1. about — Side facts, wide paragraph (table)
Two rows share one vertical line at 34% of the viewport width. Row one: in a 160px column at the left margin, three label and value pairs stack with 20px between them, each a label in the soft ink with its value beneath in full ink, all at 13px (founded, based in, team size). From the 34% line a five-line paragraph at clamp(20px, 1.7vw, 26px), leading 1.35, runs to 90% of the width. Row two starts 120px lower and repeats the logic for the record of the practice: one plain word at 13px at the far left (awards, press, clients), then from the 34% line two list columns, each 260px wide with a 64px gutter, at 13px with 10px between lines: name at the left, year at the right in the soft ink. No heading; the paragraph opens the section. Each list comfortably holds 6–12 lines. No photographs are needed.
On a phone: Everything aligns to the left margin. The paragraph comes first at 19px. The three label and value pairs follow as a two-column grid at 14px. The two record lists stack with 40px between them, each opened by its plain word in full ink.

### 2. work — Oversized visual, small cluster (split)
One project per block, three or four blocks, 140px apart. In each, one oversized visual fills columns 1–7 and bleeds off the left viewport edge: an object photograph scaled to 120% of its box and cropped by `overflow: hidden` on the section, 80svh tall, sitting on the page ground (use `mix-blend-mode: multiply` on a light ground so a white studio background disappears). The right third, columns 9–12, holds a small cluster bottom-aligned with the visual: a landscape image at 16:10 with 6px corners, then 28px below it a two-column row with the project title at clamp(28px, 3vw, 44px) on two lines at the left and a five-line description at 13–14px at the right. Column 8 stays empty. Blocks do not mirror; the second visual is 60svh tall and the third 90svh, so the rhythm changes while the structure holds. No section heading: each title leads its block.
On a phone: The visual comes first at 110% of the width, bleeding off the left edge in a 4:5 crop. Beneath it the title at 8vw, then the description at 15px, then the small landscape image at 70% width against the right margin. 88px between blocks.

### 3. gallery — Staggered pairs in empty ground (stack)
No heading. Photographs are placed one or two per screen so that more than half of every screen stays empty ground. Screen one: a 3:4 portrait in columns 1–4 against the left margin, and a near-square 5:4 image in columns 7–12 against the right margin whose top edge starts 30vh lower than the portrait's. Screen two: a 3:2 landscape in columns 6–11 and, 24vh lower, a small 4:5 image in columns 2–4. Repeat the two arrangements, mirrored on the second pass, for six to ten photographs. Under each image's left corner, a one-line caption at 13px in the soft ink, 10px below the image. Vertical gaps between pairs are 22–28vh. Every edge sits on a column line and offsets come from only two values. Square corners. If motion is used, images enter with a short `clip-path` wipe from the bottom edge.
On a phone: A single column in which widths and sides alternate: 70% against the left margin, 84% against the right, 56% left, then 100% full-bleed, with 64px between images. Captions sit under each image's left corner at 13px. Portrait crops are preferred so each image fills most of the screen height.

### 4. contact — The email as display type (statement)
The section is 100svh in the inverse of the page's colours. A single email address is set as one line of display type spanning the content width: the font-size is fitted so the address runs from margin to margin (about 7–9vw for 20 characters; break after the @ onto two lines if it is longer than 26), light or regular weight, tight tracking, sitting on a baseline 12vh above the bottom edge. It is a `mailto:` link; on hover the whole line takes the accent ink. In the top-left corner, a sentence of up to 12 words at 20–22px says what to write about. In the two bottom corners at 14px: phone and city at the left, two or three social links at the right. No heading and no form. For a pinned ending, make the section `position: sticky; bottom: 0` beneath the page so the previous section lifts off it.
On a phone: The address breaks after the @ onto two lines at 11.5vw, left-aligned, sitting at 55% of an 86svh screen. The sentence sits at the top-left at 18px. Phone, city and social links stack at the bottom-left at 15px with 12px between them.

## 8. Motion — Masked headline
On load, each line of the headline rises from behind its own baseline, about 900ms with a strong ease-out, the second line 110ms after the first; the supporting sentence and the action then fade up together. Nothing else on the page performs. Because headlines are two lines at most, you author the line breaks and wrap each line yourself. Recipe: 'Masked line reveal' in references/motion.md.
This is the signature move, the one loud moment. Around it the page still needs the rest of the motion layer (SKILL.md step 9): an entrance for the parts of every section, feedback on every control, and one moving picture. The inspector fails a page that barely moves. Recipes: references/motion.md.

## 10. Where the richness comes from
Light against dark, and the slow pacing.

## 11. How this direction goes wrong
Grey text on black fails contrast quickly: check it. No glow effects, no neon accent, no coloured light blobs. A dark page with a bright busy photograph is not this direction; choose low-key images. Dark does not mean empty: every screen still carries its content at a readable size, and running text is a light ink at 7:1 or more, not a dim grey.

## Always
Headlines two lines or fewer at every width. No eyebrow labels, no monospace, no 01/02/03, no thin rules, no gradients, no shadows at rest,
no cream-and-terracotta, no violet. The frame: one h1 on the first screen that says what this is, equal outer margins, no
section huddled on one side with the other half empty, no blank stretch over half a screen, a heading on every section.
`inspect.mjs` measures all of it.
